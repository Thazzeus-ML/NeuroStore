## Summary

`nsharness` is the Colab benchmark turned into a domain-neutral harness: one featurizer, five interchangeable stores (exact, IVF with optional 8-bit vectors, FlyHash, Hopfield, VSA), seven standardized corruption families, OOD and chimera tests, and latency, throughput and deployed-bytes accounting. Its first domain is predictive maintenance on NASA C-MAPSS. The protocol and the gate rows below were committed before any real-data result existed; {{n_gates_held}} of {{n_gates}} gate rows came out HELD, and the rest are reported as they are.

**What C-MAPSS can and cannot say.** It is a simulation. Its sensor noise is simulated and every corruption in the sweeps was injected by the harness, so nothing here measures natural field corruption (dead sensors, real drift, gaps). That needs real data; NASA's IMS bearing run-to-failure set is the obvious next one. Everything below ran on one CPU thread with NumPy; no accelerator was measured.

**Findings**

1. **Retrieval is a competent RUL estimator (G2 {{verdict_G2}}).** On the FD001 official test the exact Euclidean store has RMSE {{rmse_ref}} cycles (95 % CI {{rmse_ref_ci}}), against {{rmse_const}} for a constant predictor and {{rmse_ridge}} for ridge regression on the same features. It stays ahead of ridge and the constant on FD002 to FD004 (reference RMSE {{rmse_exact_l2_FD002}}, {{rmse_exact_l2_FD003}}, {{rmse_exact_l2_FD004}}), though on FD003 its edge over ridge is inside the noise (ratio {{ref_vs_ridge_FD003}}, CI {{ref_vs_ridge_FD003_ci}}).
2. **The similarity function mattered more than the storage format.** Cosine exact is {{cos_over_l2_test}} times worse than Euclidean exact on the official test (RMSE {{rmse_exact}} against {{rmse_ref}}; cross-validated {{cv_exact}} against {{cv_l2}}) because cosine discards magnitude on z-scored statistics features. I only noticed because a smoke test on fake data showed "VSA beats exact". The study therefore carries both and takes the better one as the reference (P6 {{pred_P6}}), so that the comparisons below are about the architecture and not about the metric.
3. **What keeps up with exact (G3, margin 10 %).** Hopfield attention with the same similarity has ratio {{ratio_hopfield}} ({{verdict_G3_hopfield}}): it is the exact store with a softmax kernel, same accuracy and same bytes. IVF has ratio {{ratio_ann}} and IVF with 8-bit vectors {{ratio_ann_sq8}} (verdicts {{verdict_G3_ann}} and {{verdict_G3_ann_sq8}}: the point estimates sit inside the margin and the upper confidence bounds just outside it). VSA has ratio {{ratio_vsa}} ({{verdict_G3_vsa}}: it misses the margin on FD001 by a hair, and is within a few percent of the reference on FD002 to FD004). FlyHash has ratio {{ratio_flyhash}} ({{verdict_G3_flyhash}}); it was behind the reference on every sub-dataset, and its best cross-validated configuration was the smallest and sparsest code in the grid.
4. **Cost (G4 {{verdict_G4}}).** Deployed bytes as a multiple of the exact store's: IVF with 8-bit vectors {{bytes_x_ann_sq8}}, FlyHash {{bytes_x_flyhash}}, VSA {{bytes_x_vsa}}. P1 (outcome {{pred_P1}}) guessed that neither sparse code would be smaller than the exact store, since an item is only 56 floats; that held for VSA, but FlyHash is small because cross-validation picked the sparsest code in its grid, and that code is also the one with the largest loss against exact. The memory half of G4 is met by IVF with 8-bit vectors, whose G3 verdict is {{verdict_G3_ann_sq8}}, which the pre-registered G4 rule accepts. For one query at 20 thousand items IVF is not faster than brute force (p50 {{p50_ann}} ms against {{p50_exact_l2}} ms); at {{scale_big_n}} clustered synthetic vectors it is {{scale_speedup_ann}} times faster at recall@10 {{scale_recall_ann_500000}} (those clusters are easy, real data may need more probes).
5. **Robustness (G5 {{verdict_G5}}).** At the middle severity level (L50, calibrated on the reference, see the table of severities), VSA degrades significantly less than the reference in {{n_better_vsa}} of {{n_families}} families, and its absolute RMSE under corruption is lower in {{n_abs_better_vsa}} of {{n_families}} even though its clean RMSE is worse. FlyHash degrades less in {{n_better_flyhash}} families, but from a worse starting point, so read the ratio table next to the absolute table. Hopfield and IVF degrade more than the reference (worse in {{n_worse_hopfield}} and {{n_worse_ann}} families); one plausible cause for IVF is that a corrupted query is assigned to the wrong cells, which I have not tested. Why VSA is robust is also untested here.
6. **The store has to be told which sensors are dead (G7 {{verdict_G7}}).** With the observed-sensor mask, the reference's degradation under {{dropout_channels_L50}} lost channels falls from {{deg_exact_l2_dropout}} to {{deg_exact_l2_mask_dropout}} times, and completion NMSE with 30 % of channels lost falls from {{nmse_exact_l2_0.3}} (worse than mean imputation, {{nmse_mean_imputation_0.3}}) to {{nmse_exact_l2_mask_0.3}}. Without the mask the zero-filled channels mislead retrieval, so an unflagged retrieval store can be worse than no memory at all. VSA is the least affected without the mask ({{nmse_vsa_0.3}}).
7. **Familiarity (G6a {{verdict_G6a}}, G6b {{verdict_G6b}}, G6c {{verdict_G6c}}).** Gaussian windows are flagged perfectly. Channel chimeras are detected better as the life-stage gap grows (AUROC {{auc_ref_chimera_channel_same}}, {{auc_ref_chimera_channel_mid}}, {{auc_ref_chimera_channel_far}} for same, mid, far), but the same-stage control is well above chance (P5 {{pred_P5}}) and time-splice chimeras are detectable even at the same stage ({{auc_ref_chimera_time_same}}), so part of the signal is a splicing artefact and not physics. A new fault mode at the same operating condition (FD003 late-life windows against an FD001 memory) is only weakly recognised ({{auc_ref_foreign_FD003_late}}, CI {{auc_ref_foreign_FD003_late_ci}}), whereas new operating conditions (FD002) are trivial ({{auc_ref_foreign_FD002}}). Cosine familiarity is blind to some chimeras: its far-gap time-splice AUROC is {{auc_exact_chimera_time_far}}, below chance. Hopfield's neg-energy score adds nothing over plain max similarity here (far-gap channel chimeras {{negE_chimera_channel_far}} against {{auc_hopfield_chimera_channel_far}}).

**What I would not conclude.** That FlyHash is poor in general (it was tried on 56 dense float features with a nine-point grid whose best point was an edge). That VSA beats exact (its clean accuracy is worse; the robustness edge is for injected corruption and its mechanism is untested). That the ANN speed-up carries over to real feature distributions. That any of this transfers to real sensors. Several selected readouts sit at the edge of the pre-registered grid (k at its largest value for every store, see the flags below), so all ratios are relative to a reference that could be tuned a little further.
